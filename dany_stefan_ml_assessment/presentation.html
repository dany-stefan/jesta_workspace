Q2: Root Cause Analysis

The difference comes from these categories having fundamentally different product, sales, and demand patterns. Footwear addresses one body part, whereas made-to-measure can dress your whole body with products. This affects the modeling of these categories.

Footwear: High-velocity with many SKUs, frequent stockouts, aggressive markdowns, and strong seasonality. Inventory takes a hit with some variations (e.g., color) more popular than others; thus, censored demand problems arise where out-of-stock does not mean low demand.

Made-to-measure: Low-velocity, where the inventory pipeline is more controlled and prices stay fixed, as it caters to a niche audience with custom orders. Demand is more predictable and less affected by stockouts or markdowns. The model's error is lower because the data is cleaner, less censored, and less affected by external factors.

Investigations: Stockout frequency, SKU churn on active products or less popular products, markdown events and their frequency, seasonality, and traffic on websites measuring abandonment rates.

⚡ Q3: Week 1 Critical Decision - Quick Win Priority

Decision: Set up a window of replenishable time—if the product saw a restock in the last x days, then we mark it as an active catalog product. This means our subset has active, in-stock, replenishable products with sales history.

✅ Why This First?

  • Immediately fixes OOM crashes, enables demos, and reveals model performance improvements
  • For business folks, show progress—tackling memory issue first shows quick result time
  • Unlocks downstream wait times and holdups
  • Dead stock is the quickest win, with a window variable we can set and tune later

📥📤 Task 3: System Design - Inputs/Outputs

📥 Detailed Inputs Description:

  • Raw Sales Data: Historical sales data (90-120 days) to identify active products and patterns in SKU lifecycle
  • Inventory Levels: Current stock status to determine availability and distinguish genuine dead stock from temporary stockouts
  • Product Metadata: Category, price, seasonality flags, channel (online/in-store) - understand demand drivers and where product exists in real life
  • Stocking History: Current levels showing which products get restocked regularly and ones that can be replenished
  • Replenishment Patterns: Planned orders (next 60-90 days) to identify active restocking
  • Promotional Event Data: To capture demand spikes tied to sales, holidays, markdown events

📤 Detailed Outputs Description:

  • Cleaned Dataset: Only active, replenishable products with reduced noise from irrelevant SKUs
  • Engineered Features: Stockout indicators, price elasticity metrics, seasonality flags
  • Normalized Data: Categorical converted to numerical, lag features for stockouts/price changes, interaction features (price × seasonality)
  • Format: Compatible with existing modeling pipeline, normally distributed, limited skewness and outliers
  • Storage: Data lake or database accessible by modeling component for training and forecasting

This component sits in the ETL pipeline as a data processing and feature engineering module before the modeling step. It takes raw data, filters inactive products, engineers relevant features, and outputs a cleaned dataset ready for model training. 📋 Task 3: Business Rules by Channel & Category

📋 Business Rules Context:

Different channels and categories require tailored qualification logic due to fundamentally different sales patterns, inventory constraints, and demand dynamics. The Product Qualification component applies these rules to ensure only active, replenishable, relevant products are forecasted.

Channel Category Sales Lookback Replenishment Window Rationale
Physical Retail Standard 90 days 60 days Higher carrying costs, shelf space constraints → Shorter lookback to identify active products
Footwear (High velocity) 60 days 30 days Fast fashion cycles, frequent stockouts, aggressive markdowns → Tighter window for agile filtering
E-commerce Standard 120 days 90 days Lower storage costs, no shelf space limits, volatile demand (flash sales, digital marketing) → Longer tail for online products
Made-to-Measure 120 days 90 days Custom orders, niche audience, stable pricing, longer lead times → Longer window for predictable demand patterns

📦 High-Volume Categories (e.g., Groceries):

  • Lookback: 30-45 days (capture recent velocity)
  • Include: ≥10 sales/week in last 30 days
  • Include: Restocked ≥2× in last 60 days
  • Include: Top 20% by sales/revenue in category
  • Exclude: No sales in last 30 days
  • Exclude: Not restocked in last 60 days
  • Exclude: Negative margins or high return rates
  • Monitor: Stockout periods frequently (crucial for high-velocity SKUs driving revenue)

💎 Low-Volume Categories (e.g., Luxury):

  • Lookback: 90-180 days (capture niche demand)
  • Include: ≥1 sale/week avg in last 90 days
  • Include: Restocked in last 60 days
  • Include: Brand identity products (pride items)
  • Include: Strategic for upsell/cross-sell (even if not forecasted directly)
  • Exclude: Not restocked in 60 days + declining velocity
  • Exclude: Disrupts supply chain or low margins
  • Monitor: Custom orders, pre-order unique patterns, less frequent due to stable demand

🆕 New Products (within 60 days):

  • Lookback: 30-45 days (grace period)
  • Include: ≥3 sales or 10 web sessions in first 30 days
  • Include: Restocked ≥1× since launch
  • Exclude: Fails launch sales objectives or not restocked after initial launch
  • Flag: Limited editions, marketing campaigns
  • Monitor: Frequent adjustments due to high volatility and limited history

📊 Mature Products (>60 days):

  • Lookback: 90-180 days (comprehensive trends)
  • Include: ≥1 sale/week avg in last 90 days
  • Include: Restocked in last 60 days
  • Exclude: Not restocked in 60 days or declining velocity
  • Monitor: Seasonal fluctuations, market changes, competitor actions
  • Review: Category performance (growing vs declining segment)

Decision Logic (Implemented in Polars):

def qualify_product( sales_history: pl.DataFrame, replenishment: pl.DataFrame, product_meta: pl.DataFrame, channel: str, category: str ) -> pl.DataFrame: """ Determine if product should be forecasted Returns: DataFrame with columns: - product_id, site_id - is_qualified: True/False - reason: explanation string - confidence: 0.0-1.0 """ # Get appropriate windows windows = get_qualification_windows(channel, category) # Rule 1: Sales Recency recent_sales = ( sales_history .filter(pl.col('date') >= pl.col('date').max() - pl.duration(days=windows['sales_lookback'])) .group_by('product_id', 'site_id') .agg(pl.col('sales').sum().alias('recent_sales')) ) # Rule 2: Replenishment Planned upcoming_replenishment = ( replenishment .filter(pl.col('order_date') <= pl.col('order_date').max() + pl.duration(days=windows['repl_window'])) .group_by('product_id', 'site_id') .agg(pl.col('quantity').sum().alias('planned_qty')) ) # Rule 3: Profitability Check (margin > 10%) profitable = product_meta.filter((pl.col('current_price') - pl.col('cost')) / pl.col('current_price') > 0.10) # Combine rules qualified = ( recent_sales .join(upcoming_replenishment, on=['product_id', 'site_id'], how='left') .join(profitable, on='product_id', how='inner') .with_columns([ pl.when((pl.col('recent_sales') > 0) | (pl.col('planned_qty') > 0)) .then(True) .otherwise(False) .alias('is_qualified'), pl.when((pl.col('recent_sales') > 0) & (pl.col('planned_qty') > 0)) .then('Active with replenishment') .when(pl.col('recent_sales') > 0) .then('Active but no replenishment (review)') .otherwise('Inactive') .alias('reason') ]) ) return qualified

⚖️ Task 3: Scale (150K SKUs × 61 stores) & Validation

🚀 Handling Scale Without OOM:

Problem: 150,000 SKUs × 61 stores = 9.15M product-store combinations. With 90-day history, that's ~820M rows.

Solution: Chunked Parallel Processing

from multiprocessing import Pool import polars as pl def process_chunk(chunk_params): """Process one chunk of products""" skus, start_date, end_date = chunk_params # Load only needed data (lazy evaluation) sales = ( pl.scan_parquet('sales/*.parquet') .filter(pl.col('product_id').is_in(skus)) .filter(pl.col('date').is_between(start_date, end_date)) .collect() ) # Apply qualification logic qualified = qualify_product(sales, ...) return qualified # Split into chunks (10K SKUs per chunk) chunk_size = 10_000 chunks = [product_ids[i:i+chunk_size] for i in range(0, len(product_ids), chunk_size)] # Parallel processing (4 workers) with Pool(processes=4) as pool: results = pool.map(process_chunk, chunks) # Combine results final_qualified = pl.concat(results)

✅ Comprehensive Validation Strategy:

Validation Approach: Combine operational metrics (data engineering POV) + business metrics (forecasting POV) to ensure the Product Qualification component works effectively

Metric 1: Qualification Rate by Category

  • Target: 30-50% of products qualified overall (balanced filtering)
  • Monitor by category: Footwear ~30%, Made-to-Measure ~60%
  • Alert if rate suddenly drops >10% (signals data quality issue or business change)
  • Purpose: Identify if certain categories are being over or under-filtered

Metric 2: False Negative Rate (FN)

  • Track products excluded by qualification logic but later recorded sales (missed opportunities)
  • Target: <10% false negatives
  • Monthly review with merchandising SMEs: adjust rules if FN rate trends up
  • Keep discarded items log to see if any start selling again in the future (refine logic over time)
  • Risk: High FN indicates filtering criteria too strict

Metric 3: False Positive Rate (FP)

  • Track products included in forecasting dataset but never recorded any sales (wasted compute)
  • Target: <10% false positives
  • Calculate: (qualified but zero sales in next 30 days) / total qualified
  • Risk: High FP suggests filtering criteria too lenient

Metric 4: Downstream WMAPE Impact

  • Most important: Does qualification improve forecast accuracy?
  • A/B test: Old system (all products) vs New (qualified only)
  • Expected: WMAPE reduction 15-20 percentage points
  • Measure by category: ensure improvements across Footwear, Made-to-Measure, etc.

Operational Metrics:

  • Reduction in product count: 12.6M → 5M (60% reduction)
  • Peak memory usage: 64 GB → 8 GB per worker (chunked processing)
  • Processing time: 45 min → 12 min (parallel processing)
  • OOM crashes: Eliminated (from frequent to none)
  • Error rates: Data corruption checks, row count validation

Validation Processes:

  • Manual Audits: Regularly review sample of qualified and excluded products with SMEs to ensure logic aligns with business expectations
  • SME Reviews: Include merchandising experts to review excluded items list, reinforce completeness of A/B testing
  • Alert Setup: Trigger if sudden spike in FN/FP, or abnormal processing time/memory usage (signals inefficiencies or bugs)
  • Continuous Refinement: Regularly review metrics and adjust filtering criteria, quarterly adjustments by category and channel

Q1: Why did you prioritize Product Qualification in Week 1?

Answer: It’s the highest-leverage fix.

  • Removes dead stock noise that inflates WMAPE.
  • Cuts compute and memory usage, eliminating OOM crashes.
  • Improves signal quality so downstream models actually learn demand.

Trade-off: Delays model tuning by ~1–2 weeks, but avoids optimizing on bad data.

Q2: How do you avoid excluding products that still matter?

Answer: Use a review bucket and monitor false negatives.

  • Borderline SKUs (recent sales but no replenishment) → manual review.
  • Monthly FN tracking: excluded products that later sell.
  • Adjust thresholds quarterly by category and channel.

Goal: Keep FN rate below 10% while still removing dead stock noise.

Q3: How will you validate progress in the first 30 days?

Answer: Focus on operational + accuracy metrics.

  • Qualified product rate by category (target 30–50%).
  • Runtime + memory (OOM eliminated, batch time reduced).
  • WMAPE improvement on qualified SKUs vs baseline.

Method: Shadow mode A/B test before enabling new forecasts in production.

✅ Feature 1: Stockout Detection (Implemented)

def detect_stockout(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame: """ Flag periods where inventory=0 but recent sales existed Logic: If inventory hits 0 and sales in prior 7 days > 0, this is a stockout (unmet demand), not end of lifecycle """ df = df.copy() # Calculate 7-day rolling sales before current day df['sales_7d_lag'] = df['sales'].shift(1).rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).sum() # Stockout flag: inventory=0 AND recent sales existed df['is_stockout'] = ( (df['inventory'] == 0) & (df['sales_7d_lag'] > 0) ).astype(int) # Count consecutive stockout days df['stockout_duration'] = df.groupby( (df['is_stockout'] != df['is_stockout'].shift(1)).cumsum() )['is_stockout'].cumsum() return df # Example from Day 41-50 stockout period # Day 40: inventory=10, sales=3 → sales_7d_lag=21 # Day 41: inventory=0, sales=0 → is_stockout=1 (unmet demand!) # Days 41-50: All flagged as stockout

Impact: Model learns to impute demand during stockout periods instead of treating zeros as "no demand".

✅ Feature 2: Product Lifecycle (Implemented)

from datetime import datetime, timedelta def calculate_lifecycle_stage(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame: """ Classify products into: New, Growth, Mature, Clearance Logic: - New: <30 days since launch - Growth: Sales trending up (7d > 28d MA) - Mature: Stable sales, no markdown - Clearance: Price dropped >20% from original """ df = df.copy() # Weeks since launch df['date'] = pd.to_datetime(df['date']) df['launch_date'] = pd.to_datetime(df['launch_date']) df['weeks_since_launch'] = ((df['date'] - df['launch_date']).dt.days / 7).astype(int) # Sales momentum (7d vs 28d) df['sales_ma_7'] = df['sales'].rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).mean() df['sales_ma_28'] = df['sales'].rolling(window=28, min_periods=1).mean() df['sales_momentum'] = df['sales_ma_7'] - df['sales_ma_28'] # Markdown depth df['markdown_pct'] = 1 - (df['current_price'] / df['original_price']) # Lifecycle stage classification df['stage'] = 'Unknown' df.loc[df['weeks_since_launch'] < 4, 'stage'] = 'New' df.loc[(df['weeks_since_launch'] >= 4) & (df['sales_momentum'] > 0) & (df['markdown_pct'] < 0.10), 'stage'] = 'Growth' df.loc[(df['weeks_since_launch'] >= 4) & (abs(df['sales_momentum']) <= 1) & (df['markdown_pct'] < 0.10), 'stage'] = 'Mature' df.loc[df['markdown_pct'] >= 0.20, 'stage'] = 'Clearance' # One-hot encode df = pd.get_dummies(df, columns=['stage'], prefix='stage', drop_first=False) return df # Example: Day 70 markdown starts → stage switches from Mature to Clearance

Impact: Model adapts predictions based on lifecycle stage (e.g., expect higher sales variability in Growth, surge in Clearance).

def calculate_sales_momentum(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame: """ Compare recent trend (7-day) vs longer trend (28-day) Positive momentum = accelerating sales Negative momentum = decelerating sales """ df = df.copy() # Moving averages with 1-day shift (prevent leakage) df['sales_ma_7'] = df['sales'].shift(1).rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).mean() df['sales_ma_28'] = df['sales'].shift(1).rolling(window=28, min_periods=1).mean() # Momentum: difference between short-term and long-term trend df['sales_momentum'] = df['sales_ma_7'] - df['sales_ma_28'] # Momentum direction (categorical) df['momentum_direction'] = 'Stable' df.loc[df['sales_momentum'] > 1.0, 'momentum_direction'] = 'Accelerating' df.loc[df['sales_momentum'] < -1.0, 'momentum_direction'] = 'Decelerating' # Rate of change df['sales_roc'] = df['sales_ma_7'].pct_change() return df # Example interpretation: # Day 60: sales_ma_7=3.2, sales_ma_28=2.8 → momentum=+0.4 (Growth) # Day 75 (post-markdown): sales_ma_7=6.5, sales_ma_28=3.1 → momentum=+3.4 (Surge!)

Impact: Captures acceleration/deceleration trends that simple lags miss. Critical for detecting markdown-driven spikes.

✅ Feature 4: Inventory Health (Implemented)

def calculate_inventory_health(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame: """ Calculate: days of supply, markdown depth, margin status Days of Supply = inventory / avg daily sales (measures overstocking) Margin Status = healthy (>30%), warning (10-30%), critical (<10%) """ df = df.copy() # Average daily sales (trailing 28 days) df['avg_daily_sales'] = df['sales'].rolling(window=28, min_periods=1).mean() # Days of supply (how many days current inventory will last) df['days_of_supply'] = df['inventory'] / (df['avg_daily_sales'] + 1e-6) # Avoid div by zero df['days_of_supply'] = df['days_of_supply'].clip(upper=365) # Cap outliers # Inventory trend (7-day and 28-day) df['inventory_ma_7'] = df['inventory'].rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).mean() df['inventory_ma_28'] = df['inventory'].rolling(window=28, min_periods=1).mean() df['inventory_trend_7'] = df['inventory'].diff().rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).mean() df['inventory_trend_28'] = df['inventory'].diff().rolling(window=28, min_periods=1).mean() # Markdown depth (from original price) df['markdown_depth'] = 1 - (df['current_price'] / df['original_price']) df['markdown_depth'] = df['markdown_depth'].clip(lower=0) # No negative markdowns # Margin status df['margin_pct'] = (df['current_price'] - df['cost']) / df['current_price'] df['margin_status'] = 'Healthy' df.loc[(df['margin_pct'] >= 0.10) & (df['margin_pct'] < 0.30), 'margin_status'] = 'Warning' df.loc[df['margin_pct'] < 0.10, 'margin_status'] = 'Critical' return df # Example: Day 75 (clearance) # inventory=50, avg_daily_sales=6 → days_of_supply=8.3 (healthy) # markdown_depth=0.35 (35% off) → margin_status='Warning'

Impact: Helps model understand inventory pressure → Predicts clearance events and urgency to sell.

🧠 Judgment Calls: Engineering Choices

Pragmatic decisions to keep signal high and leakage low

1) Prevent Leakage First

Call: Shift lagged features by 1 day and separate train/test by time.

Why better: Forces realistic evaluation and avoids inflated WMAPE.

Alternative: Random split → misleading performance.

2) Favor Interpretable Features

Call: Use lifecycle, stockout, and markdown signals before complex models.

Why better: Stakeholders can understand why forecasts change.

Alternative: Black-box model with unclear drivers.

3) Polars for Production

Call: Implement in Polars to match pipeline, keep Pandas for quick exploration.

Why better: Lower memory footprint and faster batch processing.

Alternative: Pandas-only → slower and harder to scale.

Principle

In production forecasting, correctness and explainability beat micro-optimizations.

📊 Task 5: Where Custom Features Helped Most

🔍 Analysis: What Worked and What Didn't

✅ What Worked:

  • Inventory features dominated (63.7% importance): Current inventory is the single best predictor. When inventory=0, sales=0 (obvious but critical).
  • Price/markdown features: During Days 86-95 (markdown period), custom features reduced error from 185% → 68% (117 point improvement!). Baseline/lags couldn't capture price elasticity.
  • Lifecycle stage classification: Switching from "Mature" to "Clearance" stage on Day 70+ told model to expect higher, more variable sales.
  • Stockout flag: Prevented model from learning "demand=0" during Days 41-50. Imputed censored demand improved accuracy in recovery period.

❌ What Didn't Work:

  • Lag features alone: Underfit regime changes. 28-day MA is too slow to react to markdown-driven demand surges (Days 70+).
  • Weekday feature (5.2% importance): Lower than expected. Might need more data to capture day-of-week patterns reliably (100 days = only 14 weeks).
  • Sales momentum: Helped but not dramatically. Likely overshadowed by inventory features (inventory controls sales ceiling).

🚀 What Next:

  • 1. More data: 100 days is insufficient for seasonality. Need 1-2 years to capture year-over-year patterns (back-to-school, holidays, etc.).
  • 2. External features: Add weather (footwear sales spike in rain), holidays, competitor pricing, fashion trends.
  • 3. Category-specific models: Train separate models for Footwear (high velocity) vs Made-to-Measure (stable demand). One-size-fits-all underperforms.
  • 4. Ensemble approach: Combine GBR (captures non-linearity) with ARIMA (captures seasonality) for better overall accuracy.

❓ Part 2 Q&A: Engineering Deep-Dive

Q1: Why use WMAPE and a time-based split?

Answer:

  • WMAPE: Matches business impact by weighting errors by actual volume.
  • Time split: Prevents leakage and mimics real forecasting (train on past, test on future).

Trade-off: WMAPE can be harsh on low-sales periods, so I also track MAE and period-specific error.

Q2: How did you treat missing data and stockouts?

Answer: I separated data quality from true demand signals.

  • Missing sales on Day 37 → imputed to 0 because inventory was 0.
  • Stockout periods flagged to avoid learning “zero demand.”
  • Sales spikes during markdowns treated as regime changes.

Impact: Prevents the model from learning bad zeros and improves post-replenishment forecasts.

Q3: Why Polars + Gradient Boosting?

Answer:

  • Polars: Matches production constraint, lower memory footprint, faster batch ops.
  • GBR: Handles non-linearity, gives feature importance, and is stable on small datasets.

Trade-off: LightGBM is faster but adds dependency complexity; GBR is sufficient for the sample.
